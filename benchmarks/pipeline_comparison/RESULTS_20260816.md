These are same-host, process-separated, one-way measurements. They are not
cross-host measurements and are not a production qualification result.

## Compared pipelines

All runs use six independent processes and five hops:

```text
Perception -> Prediction -> Planning -> Control -> Guardian -> CANBus
```

Every stage receives/resolves, converts to the shared semantic frame, validates
all scalar fields and every payload byte, updates its stage timestamp, and
publishes to the next stage.

| Backend | Serialization/message | Transport |
|---|---|---|
| Fast DDS | bounded DDS IDL typed sample | reliable, volatile, synchronous, bounded KEEP_ALL; default UDP discovery plus intrahost SHM/data sharing automatic |
| Protobuf + ZMQ | generated Protobuf message | native ZeroMQ IPC PUSH/PULL |
| Mino | fixed-layout typed SHM frame | production SharedMemorySegment + CentralSlabAllocator + five SPSC channels + Publisher/Subscriber |

The default nominal buffering parameter is 64 for all backends, but the
semantics are not identical: Mino has an exact ring capacity, Fast DDS has
bounded reader/writer histories, and ZeroMQ HWM is a per-pipe approximation.
The Mino result uses a benchmark-static channel manifest; it does not claim to
measure cross-process Bus discovery or SharedMemoryRegion supervisor lifecycle.

## Paced end-to-end latency

Profile rates were selected to avoid sustained queue buildup:

| Profile | Payload | Rate | Measured samples/round | Rounds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| small | 256 B | 100 Hz | 1,000 | 3 |
| medium | 64 KiB | 20 Hz | 200 | 3 |
| large | 1 MiB | 10 Hz | 100 | 3 |

Values below are the median of the three per-round percentile values.

### Small — 256 B at 100 Hz

| Backend | p50 | p95 | p99 | p99.9 | Effective rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mino SHM | **19.262 us** | **21.127 us** | **23.039 us** | **27.623 us** | 100.10 Hz |
| Fast DDS | 443.326 us | 458.341 us | 466.982 us | 474.378 us | 100.10 Hz |
| Protobuf + ZMQ | 586.298 us | 609.865 us | 621.656 us | 644.285 us | 100.10 Hz |

At p50, Mino was about 23.0x lower latency than Fast DDS and 30.4x lower than
Protobuf + ZMQ. At p99, the factors were about 20.3x and 27.0x respectively.
The small profile has 1,000 samples per round, so nearest-rank p99.9 has the
minimum required sample count.

## Reliability fixes discovered by the campaign

The first high-count campaign correctly failed and was retained. It found:

1. `RELIABLE + KEEP_LAST(64)` in Fast DDS could overwrite unconsumed samples.
   The benchmark now uses bounded `KEEP_ALL`, which applies backpressure rather
   than replacing an unacknowledged sample.
2. ZeroMQ `linger=0` could discard accepted frames when an upstream stage exited
   with a non-empty local queue. Shutdown linger is now bounded by the process
   deadline so accepted frames flush without an unbounded wait.
3. ZeroMQ HWM was initially 4096 while DDS/Mino used a nominal value of 64. All
   default nominal parameters are now 64 to reduce configuration-induced bias;
   their internal buffering semantics remain explicitly non-equivalent.

The failed evidence remains in `.cache/pipeline-comparison-20260816/` and was
not overwritten.

## Limitations

- Fast DDS type support is mechanically reproduced from Fast DDS-Gen 4.2.0
  templates and upstream Fast DDS 3.4.2 generated references because the Gradle
  distribution download timed out. A fixed JDK 17 regeneration diff is required
  before formal publication.
- Fast DDS 3.4.2 is benchmark-only and compiled as C++17; Mino benchmark code
  remains C++20.
- Protobuf resolves to `29.0-rc3` through the current Bazel module graph.
- Processes were not CPU-pinned and the host was not an isolated real-time
  environment.
- The benchmark measures application construction, full validation, copying,
  serialization, middleware, scheduling and queueing across five hops. It is not
  a serialization-only microbenchmark.
- p99.9 is only statistically interpretable for the small paced profile in this
  campaign. Medium and large should use p50/p95/p99 at their recorded sample
  counts.
